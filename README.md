## Project Overview

This project analyzes India's Consumer Price Index (CPI) data using Microsoft Excel to understand price movements across different consumption categories and geographical sectors.

The analysis explores year-on-year trends, food inflation, broader consumption categories, pre- and post-COVID comparisons, and correlations between fuel prices and selected categories.

The project demonstrates practical skills in data cleaning, data transformation, Excel-based analysis, and economic data interpretation.

## ⭐ Main Highlights

- **Study period:** Monthly CPI data from **January 2013 to April 2023**.
- **Dataset coverage:** **372 original data rows** across Rural, Urban, and Rural + Urban sectors.
- **Highest year-on-year inflation in the reported comparison:** **2022**, at 6.8% Rural, 6.6% Rural + Urban, and 6.3% Urban.
- **Broader category finding:** Food accounts for **44.44%** in the project's broader-category analysis.
- **COVID-19 comparison:** Food inflation increased from **4% in 2019 to 8% in 2020**, as reported in the workbook analysis.
- **Correlation examples:** Fuel and Light has a **69% positive correlation** with non-alcoholic beverages and a **-24% negative correlation** with cereals and products in the selected analysis.
- **Excel workflow:** Data cleaning, transformation, PivotTables, PivotCharts, visual analysis, and correlation calculations.
- **Important interpretation note:** CPI index levels and inflation rates are different measures; correlation does not establish causation.

> **Note:** These highlights reproduce the findings stated in the project material. Check the underlying formulas, time periods, and chart calculations before using them as formal economic conclusions.

## Objectives

- Analyze India's CPI trends from 2013 to 2023.

## Dataset Information

The workbook contains monthly CPI observations covering **January 2013 to April 2023**.

### Dataset Dimensions

- **Time Period:** January 2013 – April 2023
- **Frequency:** Monthly
- **Geographical Sectors:** Rural, Urban, and Rural + Urban
- **Observations:** 372 data rows in the original dataset
- **Consumption Categories:** Multiple food and non-food categories, including cereals, meat and fish, milk, vegetables, fuel and light, transport and communication, education, health, and others.

### Important Variables

| Variable | Description |
|---|---|
| Sector | Geographical classification of the CPI observation |
| Year | Year of observation |
| Month | Month of observation |
| Cereals and Products | CPI index for cereals and related products |
| Meat and Fish | CPI index for meat and fish |
| Milk and Products | CPI index for milk and related products |
| Vegetables | CPI index for vegetables |
| Fruits | CPI index for fruits |
| Fuel and Light | CPI index for fuel and light |
| Transport and Communication | CPI index for transport and communication |
| Education | CPI index for education |
| Health | CPI index for health |
| General Index | Overall CPI index |

The workbook includes additional consumption categories beyond those listed above.

**Note:** CPI index values represent price levels relative to a reference period. They should not be confused with inflation rates, which measure percentage changes in prices over time.

### 2. Year-on-Year Analysis
Examines yearly CPI patterns and differences between Rural, Urban, and Rural + Urban sectors.

<p align="center">
  <img src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/c6683b99-2c27-49d4-851e-4a012bafc1d9" alt="image" width="800" style="max-width: 100%; height: auto;" />

**Key insight:** Inflation peaked in **2022** at **6.8% in Rural**, **6.6% in Rural + Urban**, and **6.3% in Urban** areas. The project reports that inflation eased in 2023; note that the dataset ends in April 2023.

1. Russia–Ukraine war: Disrupted global supplies and raised commodity prices.
2. Food shortages: Extreme heat and uneven rainfall damaged crops, increasing food prices.
3. Fuel costs: Higher crude oil prices increased transportation and production expenses.
4. Global supply disruptions: Expensive raw materials intensified price pressures. 


### 3. Monthly Food Inflation Analysis
Explores monthly variations in food-related CPI categories, including cereals, milk, fruits, vegetables, and pulses.

<p align="center">
  <img src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/347d6d14-4cbc-484d-8a68-477640bc2ec8" alt="image" width="800" style="max-width: 100%; height: auto;" />

**Key insight:** Vegetable prices showed the sharpest fluctuation, falling **12.7% in Dec-22** before rebounding to **3.5% in May-23**. Fruit prices peaked at **7.1% in Feb-23**. Milk inflation reached **1.5% in Feb-23**, while pulses rose **1.2% in May-23**.


### 4. Absolute CPI Analysis
Examines CPI index levels across consumption categories to understand how price levels vary over the study period.

<p align="center">

**Key insight:** From 2022–2023, vegetable inflation fell **13%** across Rural, Rural + Urban, and Urban sectors. Spices recorded the highest increase (**12%** in Rural and combined areas; **11%** in Urban areas), while oils and fats declined **8%, 7%, and 5%**, respectively.


### 5. Pre- and Post-COVID Comparison
Investigates changes in CPI patterns before, during, and after the COVID-19 disruption.

<p align="center">
  <img src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/4cf5ebd0-e731-41f3-b236-6512af567d92" alt="image" width="800" style="max-width: 100%; height: auto;" />

**Key insight:** Food inflation rose from **4% in 2019 to 8% in 2020**, consistent with supply-chain disruption during the pandemic. The project reports healthcare at **7% in 2019 and 4% in 2020**, while essential household services slowed from **4% to 2%**. In 2021, food was **5%** and healthcare **8%**.


### 6. Fuel and Other Category Relationships
Examines relationships between Fuel and Light and selected categories, including transport, education, food, cereals, and non-alcoholic beverages.

  #### 6.1 Fuel vs Non-Alcoholic Beverages (69%): Strong positive correlation. Beverage prices closely track fuel costs, with energy hikes translating into higher   beverage inflation, especially in 2021 and 2022.

  <p align="center">
  <img src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/3dabd0a9-4f26-47f9-abf8-f547c6cb9644" alt="image" width="800" style="max-width: 100%; height: auto;" />

  **Key insight:** From 2021 to 2023, Fuel and Light showed a **strong positive correlation of 69%** with non-alcoholic beverages. Fuel hikes in early 2021 and April 2022 aligned with  beverage inflation, while late 2022 onward reflected steady parallel growth, underscoring energy’s significant influence on beverage costs.


  #### 6.2 Fuel vs Cereals & Products (-24%): Weak Negative Correlation. Rising fuel costs coincided with declining cereal prices, highlighting that staple food  inflation was not directly tied to energy fluctuations.

  **Key insight:** From 2021 to 2023, Fuel and Light showed a **weak negative correlation of -24%** with cereals and products. Rising fuel costs often coincided with declining   cereal prices, highlighting an inverse relationship where energy inflation did not directly drive staple food inflation.

- Correlation does not prove causation.
